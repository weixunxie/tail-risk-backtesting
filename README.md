# Comparative Tail Risk Analysis of the S&P 500 and CSI 300

A quantitative risk analytics project comparing the downside tail-risk profiles of two major
equity indices using rolling-window VaR and Expected Shortfall (CVaR) forecasts and formal out-of-sample backtests (Kupiec, Christoffersen, Basel traffic light, Acerbi-Szekely).

---

## Project Overview

This project applies Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) methodologies
to daily price data for the S&P 500 (US equities) and CSI 300 (Chinese equities) over the
period January 2010 – December 2023. Three modelling approaches are evaluated at two
confidence levels, and a rolling-window backtesting framework is used to compare the
empirical performance of each model across both markets.

The **Python implementation** is the primary version of this project. R Markdown and MATLAB
implementations are preserved as supporting/legacy versions.

---

## Research Question

> Do standard VaR and CVaR models — calibrated on Historical, Normal, and EWMA-based
> volatility estimates — perform consistently across the S&P 500 and CSI 300 when subjected
> to rolling-window backtesting? How do their downside risk profiles differ?

---

## Motivation

VaR and CVaR are foundational tools in financial risk management, widely used by banks,
asset managers, and regulators. Comparing their behaviour across two major markets with
different structural characteristics (US vs. Chinese equities) provides insight into the
portability of standard risk models and highlights where distributional assumptions may
break down.

## Methodology

### Rolling Window (strictly out-of-sample)

A 4-year estimation window (4 × 252 = 1,008 trading days) is used throughout. The forecast
for day *t* uses returns up to *t − 1* only. All models are evaluated on the same common test
window: S&P 500 2014-01-07 → 2023-12-29 (n = 2,513) and CSI 300 2014-03-10 → 2023-12-29 (n = 2,384).

### Risk Models

| Model | VaR | Expected Shortfall (CVaR) |
|---|---|---|
| **Historical Simulation** | Empirical α-quantile of window | Mean of window returns ≤ VaR |
| **Normal (Parametric)** | μ + Φ⁻¹(α) · σ from window | μ − σ · φ(Φ⁻¹(α)) / α |
| **EWMA-Normal** (λ = 0.94) | Φ⁻¹(α) · σ_EWMA,t | −σ_EWMA,t · φ(Φ⁻¹(α)) / α |

EWMA variance: σ²ₜ = λσ²ₜ₋₁ + (1 − λ)r²ₜ₋₁, seeded with the first window's sample variance.
Confidence levels: **95%** (α = 0.05) and **99%** (α = 0.01).

### VaR Backtests

| Test | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **Kupiec POF** | Unconditional coverage: are there too many **or** too few exceptions? LR ~ χ²(1) |
| **Christoffersen** | Independence: do exceptions cluster? Conditional coverage = Kupiec + independence, χ²(2) |
| **Basel traffic light (binomial)** | Green if P(X ≤ x) < 95%, Yellow if < 99.99%, Red otherwise, X ~ Bin(n, α). Penalises **risk under-estimation** |
| **Rolling 250-day Basel zones (99%)** | Regulatory rule of thumb: 0–4 Green, 5–9 Yellow, 10+ Red on every rolling 250-day window |

### Expected Shortfall Backtest

Counting days below the ES line is not a valid ES test (ES lies beyond VaR by construction).
ES is tested with the **Acerbi-Szekely (2014) Z2** statistic,
Z2 = Σ Xₜ·Iₜ / (T·α·ESₜ) + 1, whose expectation is 0 under a correct model. Negative values
mean realised tail losses exceed the forecast. P-values come from 2,000 Monte Carlo paths
simulated from each model's own forecast distribution.

> **Revision note (Sep 2026).** The first version of this project (i) compared each day's return
> with a VaR estimated from a window that already contained that day, (ii) counted EWMA exceptions
> over the full 14 years while the expected count assumed 10 years (the source of the "202
> exceptions"), (iii) took the EWMA CVaR tail mean over the *entire* sample including the future,
> (iv) used a custom traffic light that only flagged *too few* exceptions, so under-estimating
> models were labelled Green, and (v) "backtested" CVaR by counting days below the CVaR line.
> All five issues are fixed. The corrected conclusions differ materially; see below.

## Main Findings

Out-of-sample test window 2014–2023.

**VaR, 95% confidence**

| Model | S&P 500 exc. (exp. 125.7) | Kupiec p | Indep. p | CSI 300 exc. (exp. 119.2) | Kupiec p | Indep. p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Historical | 133 | 0.51 | <0.001 | 108 | 0.29 | <0.001 |
| Normal | 130 | 0.69 | <0.001 | 94 | **0.014** | <0.001 |
| EWMA | 138 | 0.27 | 0.37 | 113 | 0.56 | 0.25 |

**VaR, 99% confidence**

| Model | S&P 500 exc. (exp. 25.1) | Kupiec p | Basel | CSI 300 exc. (exp. 23.8) | Kupiec p | Basel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Historical | 35 | 0.062 | Yellow | 26 | 0.66 | Green |
| Normal | 60 | <0.001 | **Red** | 48 | <0.001 | **Red** |
| EWMA | 60 | <0.001 | **Red** | 55 | <0.001 | **Red** |

**Expected Shortfall (Acerbi-Szekely Z2, MC p-value; realised ÷ forecast tail loss)**

| Model | S&P 95% | S&P 99% | CSI 95% | CSI 99% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Historical | −0.13 (p 0.11; 1.04×) | **−0.51 (p 0.03; 1.05×)** | 0.03 (p 0.66; 1.06×) | −0.20 (p 0.31; 1.09×) |
| Normal | **−0.39 (p <0.001; 1.32×)** | **−2.31 (p <0.001; 1.36×)** | −0.04 (p 0.31; 1.33×) | **−1.64 (p <0.001; 1.32×)** |
| EWMA | **−0.35 (p <0.001; 1.19×)** | **−1.99 (p <0.001; 1.19×)** | −0.13 (p 0.08; 1.20×) | **−1.61 (p <0.001; 1.13×)** |

Bold = rejected at 5%.

1. **At 95%, coverage is fine but exceptions cluster.** Every model passes Kupiec except Normal on
   the CSI 300, which is too conservative. All sit in the Basel Green zone. But Christoffersen's
   independence test rejects Historical and Normal in both markets: a 4-year window adapts too
   slowly, so exceptions arrive in bursts. EWMA removes the clustering.
2. **At 99%, Gaussian tails fail.** Normal and EWMA-Normal VaR are breached 2.0–2.4% of the time
   against 1% nominal (Basel Red in both markets). Only Historical simulation passes, and it is
   borderline on the S&P 500.
3. **ES is under-estimated by the parametric models.** On exception days, realised losses are
   13–36% larger than the Normal/EWMA ES forecast. Z2 rejects every parametric ES at 99%.
4. **Implication.** EWMA fixes timing (clustering) but not magnitude (fat tails). Filtered
   historical simulation or Student-t innovations are the natural next step.
5. **Cross-market.** Historical simulation is Green at 99% for the CSI 300 but borderline for the
   S&P 500. The rolling Basel chart shows where each market breaks: the 2015–16 A-share crash
   for the CSI 300 and the 2020 COVID shock for the S&P 500.

## Limitations

- **Distributional assumptions** — The Normal model assumes i.i.d. Gaussian returns.
  Equity returns are typically leptokurtic with heavier tails, which may cause the Normal
  model to under-estimate tail losses at extreme confidence levels.
- **Sample-period sensitivity** — Results covering 2010–2023 include both a prolonged
  low-volatility regime and significant stress events (COVID-2020, interest-rate shock
  2022). Different sample periods would produce different backtesting outcomes.
- **Model risk** — No single VaR/CVaR model dominates across all regimes. Results should
  be interpreted alongside complementary risk measures (e.g., stress testing, scenario
  analysis).
- **Research scope** — This is an academic/educational project demonstrating quantitative
  risk methods. It is not a production risk system and does not constitute investment advice.
