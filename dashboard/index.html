Project Overview

Research Question

How do the S&P 500 and CSI 300 indices differ in their downside tail-risk profiles? Do standard VaR and CVaR models — calibrated on historical, parametric, and EWMA-based volatility estimates — perform consistently across both markets when subjected to rolling-window backtesting?

Methodology

Risk Estimation Framework

For each observation in the test period, VaR and CVaR are estimated using a trailing 4-year window (1,008 trading days). Three modelling approaches are applied at two confidence levels (95% and 99%).

Historical Simulation

Uses the empirical quantile of the rolling window to estimate VaR. CVaR is the mean of returns that fall below the VaR threshold. No distributional assumption.

Normal (Parametric)

Assumes returns within the window are normally distributed. VaR is derived from the inverse normal CDF; CVaR uses the closed-form normal tail expectation formula.

EWMA (λ = 0.94)

RiskMetrics exponentially weighted variance (σ²t = λσ²t−1 + (1−λ)r²t−1) with a zero-mean Normal VaR and ES. Adapts quickly to volatility clustering.

Formal Backtesting

Kupiec POF (coverage), Christoffersen (independence), Basel binomial traffic-light zones (penalise too many exceptions), and the Acerbi–Szekely Z2 test for Expected Shortfall. All forecasts are strictly one-day-ahead.

Backtesting

Out-of-Sample Backtest Results

Forecasts for day t use data through t−1 only; all models share the same test window (S&P 500: 2014-01-07 → 2023-12-29, n = 2,513; CSI 300: 2014-03-10 → 2023-12-29, n = 2,384). Kupiec tests whether the exception rate matches the nominal level, Christoffersen tests whether exceptions cluster, and the Basel zone flags too many exceptions (risk under-estimation) via the binomial CDF: Green < 95%, Yellow < 99.99%, Red otherwise.

Results

Key Findings

  1. 1
    At 95%, coverage is fine but exceptions cluster.
    All models pass Kupiec except Normal on the CSI 300, which is too conservative (94 vs 119 expected), and all sit in the Basel Green zone. But Christoffersen's independence test rejects Historical and Normal in both markets: a 4-year window reacts slowly, so exceptions arrive in bursts. EWMA removes the clustering.
  2. 2
    At 99%, Gaussian tails fail.
    Normal and EWMA-Normal VaR are breached 2.0–2.4% of the time against 1% nominal (Kupiec p < 0.001, Basel Red in both markets). Only Historical simulation passes: Green for the CSI 300, borderline Yellow for the S&P 500 (35 vs 25).
  3. 3
    Parametric models under-estimate Expected Shortfall.
    On exception days, realised losses are 13–36% larger than the Normal / EWMA ES forecast. The Acerbi–Szekely test rejects every parametric ES model at 99% in both markets.
  4. 4
    EWMA fixes timing, not magnitude.
    Volatility weighting removes exception clustering but keeps the thin Gaussian tail. Filtered historical simulation or Student-t innovations are the natural next step.
  5. 5
    Each market breaks in its own crisis.
    The rolling Basel chart shows the longest Red spells during the 2015–16 A-share crash for the CSI 300 and during the 2020 COVID shock for the S&P 500.

Caveats

Limitations

Distributional Assumptions The Normal model assumes i.i.d. Gaussian returns. Equity returns are typically leptokurtic with heavier tails, which can cause underestimation of extreme losses.
Sample-Period Sensitivity Results covering 2010–2023 include both a prolonged low-volatility regime and significant stress events. Different sample periods would yield different backtesting outcomes.
Model Risk No single VaR/CVaR model dominates across all regimes or confidence levels. Backtesting performance should be read alongside complementary risk measures.
Research Scope This is an academic/educational project demonstrating quantitative risk methods. Results do not constitute investment advice or a production-grade risk system.
